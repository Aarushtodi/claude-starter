# Essay rubric

## 1. Rubric first, always
- Find the prompt and rubric: the Canvas assignment page, the attached PDF, the teacher's slides, or the class note. If none is on disk and Chrome is on, read Canvas. Save both into `~/school/Notes/<Class>.md` under a dated `### Writing: <title>` heading.
- No rubric anywhere: ask him once for the handout or a photo of it. Only after that fall back to the standard format for that class (AP rubric, 5 paragraph, lab report).

## 2. Outline against the rubric
- One line per rubric row: what earns the point, where in the essay it will live.
- Thesis or claim in one sentence. If he has an idea, use his; if not, give him 3 and he picks.
- Evidence list with sources he actually has (textbook, readings, notes). No invented quotes or citations, ever.

## 3. Draft
- In his voice: a strong junior in high school, not a grad student. Plain verbs, short sentences, no em dashes, no filler openers ("Throughout history...").
- Hit the word or page count he gives. Ask if he did not.
- Mark anything he must verify with `[CHECK]`.

## 4. Self grade before he sees it
- Score the draft rubric line by rubric line as the teacher would. List what is missing.
- Fix what you can. Show him the rubric scorecard and the draft together.
- Say plainly whether it is an A draft or not.

## 5. Revision rounds
- He pastes or dictates edits. Apply them, then re-score only the rows the edit touched.
- Final: save the version he submits into the class note or a `~/school/Writing/` file, with the date.

## Honesty
- The work is his. Claude outlines, drafts, and grades; he reads it, changes it, and owns it. If his teacher bans AI for an assignment, say so and switch to outline and feedback only.
